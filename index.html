The evidence

The harm is measurable. The next wave is already arriving.

Public health bodies have documented the link between heavy social media use and poorer mental health. AI-generated video and drama now add content made at a scale, and with a precision, no human studio could match.

Pulse

Notice early. Respond gently. Escalate only when it matters.

Pulse is our concept for an early-warning tool. It is designed to sense patterns in what a teenager is exposed to, not to watch what they say.

Step 1

Sense

On the teenager’s own phone, Pulse would read how apps and games are used, and how they are rated. It does not listen to or watch their content, and nothing leaves the phone as content.

Step 2

Recognise

Patterns are compared with indicators drawn from published clinical and scientific research, such as sustained late-night use, compulsive re-opening or long hours on games rated for violence.

Step 3

Respond

The teenager hears first, with gentle prompts. Families receive a balance summary they agreed in advance. Sustained, serious patterns lead to a recommendation to seek professional support.

Our principles

Built with teenagers, not against them

Our research on adolescent privacy argues that protection works best when it respects a young person’s growing autonomy. That idea shapes every design decision.

Teenagers are told, always

Pulse would never run secretly. Young people take part knowingly, see their own insights first and know exactly what is shared.

Patterns, never private content

Parents never see messages, searches, screenshots or videos. They see trends and categories, agreed in advance.

Grounded in published research

Every signal must trace back to peer-reviewed or clinical evidence, and be validated before it is used with any family.

Support, not diagnosis

Pulse would offer early warning and guidance. It does not diagnose, treat or replace a doctor or counsellor.
