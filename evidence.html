The evidence

What the research already tells us

Teenagers’ brains are still developing the systems for reward, emotion and self-control. Platforms are built to capture attention. The evidence of harm is no longer anecdotal.

2×

Young people who spend more than three hours a day on social media face double the risk of poor mental health outcomes. Average use among adolescents surveyed was about three and a half hours a day.

US Surgeon General’s Advisory on Social Media and Youth Mental Health, May 2023 ↗
7% → 11%

Problematic social media use among adolescents rose from 7% in 2018 to 11% in 2022, across nearly 280,000 young people in 44 countries and regions.

WHO Regional Office for Europe and HBSC, A focus on adolescent social media use and gaming, September 2024 ↗
1 in 2

Nearly half of US teens aged 13 to 17 have seen AI-generated explicit material. One in four of them say it depicted themselves or someone they know.

Common Sense Media, Teens and Explicit Deepfakes in the Age of AI, July 2026 ↗
The next wave

When AI makes the content, the feed never runs out

Until recently, everything in a feed was made by someone, which set a natural limit on how much there was. Generative AI removes that limit. Video, voices and serialised short dramas can now be produced endlessly, tested against what holds attention, and tailored to an individual viewer.

For teenagers, that raises three new risks: content that is more compelling than anything made by hand, material that is false but looks real, including explicit deepfakes of people they know, and a feed with no natural end.

Parental controls built for websites and apps were not designed for this. Families need to understand patterns of exposure, not just block a list of sites.

Guidance

What health authorities advise

Singapore sets clear limits for younger children

Singapore’s Ministry of Health advises that parents should not give children aged 0 to 12 unrestricted access to mobile devices, or any access to social media services.

Singapore Ministry of Health, Guidance on Screen Use in Children, announced 21 January 2025 ↗

Psychologists call for watching the signs, not just the hours

The American Psychological Association advises monitoring adolescents for signs of problematic use that disrupt daily routines, with autonomy growing as each young person matures.

American Psychological Association, Health Advisory on Social Media Use in Adolescence, May 2023 ↗

Summaries of public sources, checked October 2026. Please read the original documents for full guidance.

The developing brain

Why adolescence matters

Adolescence is a period of rapid change in the brain’s reward and emotional systems, while the regions that support planning and self-control continue to mature into early adulthood. Our team’s systematic review of neuroimaging studies examines how digital media use relates to that development and to adolescent mental health.

Neuroplasticity and Digital Media, preprint (2025) ↗
