Pulse · in development

An early-warning pulse, designed around the teenager

Pulse is our concept for sensing harmful patterns in what reaches a young person, early enough to help. It is in development and not yet available.

What Pulse reads

Information about content, not the content itself

Pulse builds its picture from how apps and games are rated and used. It does not listen to or watch what teenagers do. What leaves the phone is a pattern signal, never content.

Apps and games, and how they are rated

Official ratings tell Pulse what kind of experience an app or game is designed to be.

  • Age ratings from the app stores
  • Game content descriptors, such as violence or gambling-like mechanics
  • Research on design features such as endless feeds and streaks

How they use them

Patterns of use, never what they write or watch.

  • Time of day and duration
  • Late-night use and compulsive re-opening
  • Rapid switching and bursts of notifications
  • Never messages, searches or images

What they choose to share

With the teenager’s agreement, platform data they download themselves, such as watch history, shows what their feeds actually served.

  • Exported by the teenager, never taken
  • Analysed for patterns, then discarded
  • Optional, and can be withdrawn at any time

Sensing content categories directly on the phone is something our research pilot will test, on Android only and with explicit consent.

The family agreement

Agreed together, visible to everyone

Pulse is set up as one of the family’s terms for having a phone. The teenager knows what they are agreeing to, and sees exactly what their parents will receive before anything is shared.

Sample agreement
  1. Pulse is installed as one of our family’s terms for having a phone.
  2. You will always know Pulse is running, and you can see exactly what we see.
  3. You hear first. We receive the weekly summary we agreed today.
  4. If a threshold is crossed, we talk about it together. It is a conversation, not a punishment.
  5. We review this agreement every six months, and your independence grows as you show you can manage it.

How it responds

Escalation that respects the teenager

Most of the time, Pulse would speak only to the teenager. It involves others step by step, and only when the evidence warrants it.

  1. 1

    The teenager first

    Private insights and gentle prompts, such as a reminder that it is late or a suggestion to step away from a feed that is affecting them.

  2. 2

    An agreed family summary

    A weekly picture of balance, trends and categories, in a format the family agreed at the start. Never the content itself.

  3. 3

    A recommendation for support

    If a serious pattern persists past thresholds defined with clinicians, the family is encouraged to speak with a doctor or counsellor. The teenager is told first.

  4. 4

    Urgent safety concerns

    Where patterns suggest risk of self-harm, the family receives immediate guidance and crisis contacts, following a protocol agreed at onboarding.

Our privacy promise

What Pulse will never do

  • Read private messages or searches
  • Listen to or watch their content
  • Show parents screenshots or videos
  • Record calls or surroundings
  • Run without the teenager knowing
  • Sell data or use it for advertising
  • Diagnose any condition
  • Let the chatbot see more than the agreement allows
Beyond your family

Pulse-LLM: evidence, a dashboard and a guide

Behind Pulse sits a research engine that knows more than any single phone can learn. It informs and explains. Validated models, signed off by clinicians, set the thresholds.

An evidence library

Clinician-reviewed research on which exposures, at what amount and timing, are linked with which outcomes. Kept current as new studies are published.

A family dashboard

Your family’s balance across sleep, attention, compulsion and connection, what each app or game is and how it is rated, plus anonymised trends from many families, such as a new app rising quickly among 14-year-olds.

A parent chatbot

Answers grounded in the evidence library, with sources. Coaching for the conversation after a threshold is crossed, and quick routes to professional help.

The chatbot sees only what the dashboard shows

It follows the family agreement, enforced by the system itself.

Answers come from the evidence

Sources are cited. When the evidence has no answer, it says so.

Trends protect every family

A pattern appears only when enough families contribute that no one can be identified.

Safety comes first

Any sign of risk of self-harm leads straight to crisis resources and human help.

A common question

Is Pulse ever hidden?

No. Pulse is designed never to run without the teenager knowing. Phone makers also build their systems to prevent hidden monitoring, and covert tools tend to backfire: teenagers who discover them switch to second accounts or devices, and the trust that keeps them safest is lost.

For younger children, the visible parental controls built into every phone are the right tool. Pulse is designed for the teenage years, when protection has to grow alongside independence.

Development roadmap

Three stages, evidence first

We will build only what the evidence supports and the platforms allow.

  1. 1

    Research pilot

    With consenting families and ethics approval: teenagers donate their own platform data, an Android research app tests content sensing, and short wellbeing check-ins show which signals matter.

  2. 2

    Pulse Patterns

    The first product, on iPhone and Android: usage patterns and app and game ratings, scored across the four harms, with teen-first prompts, the family agreement and the dashboard.

  3. 3

    Deeper signals

    Content-level signals where the pilot shows they add real value and the platforms allow it, plus data donation built into the app.

Open challenges

What we are still working out

We would rather be clear about the hard problems than pretend they are solved. These are the questions the pilot is designed to answer.

Access to the device

Phone operating systems deliberately limit what one app can see of another. That protects privacy, and it is the hardest technical problem we face.

  • Usage patterns through the phones’ own screen-time frameworks, on iPhone and Android
  • On iPhone, matching on the phone itself, because Apple keeps usage analysis on the device
  • Content sensing tested only in the Android research pilot, with explicit consent
  • Teenagers voluntarily donating their own platform data

Accuracy and validation

A wrong signal can harm trust, or worse, miss something important.

  • Validate every classifier against published research before use
  • Measure false alarms and missed signals openly in the pilot
  • Keep a person in the loop for every escalation beyond the teenager

Consent and ethics

Research with young people demands the highest standard of consent.

  • Teenager assent alongside parental consent
  • Independent ethics review before any pilot begins
  • The right to pause or leave at any time

Clinical thresholds

Deciding when a pattern matters is a clinical judgement, not a technical one.

  • Thresholds defined with clinicians and drawn from published evidence
  • Published openly so families know how decisions are made
